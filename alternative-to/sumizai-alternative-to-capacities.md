# SumizAI vs Capacities: you model the knowledge, or the model files it

Capacities is a note app built on objects instead of folders: a book, a person, a project or an idea each gets its own type, properties and backlinks, and it runs on Mac, Windows, Linux, iOS, Android and the web. SumizAI has a much smaller scope. It turns answers from your AI chats into AI notes and files them into one table of contents, on your Mac.

Both apps now involve AI, which makes the comparison less obvious than it looks. The details below come from capacities.io, its pricing page and its AI documentation, read on 23 October 2026.

## Capacities has AI too, so where is the difference?

In Capacities the AI is an assistant inside a knowledge base you design. You type `@` to pull objects into a chat, ask it to summarise a selected block, let it auto-tag or fill properties, and save a chat as an object. It picks from models by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, xAI, Mistral, DeepSeek and Moonshot, paid from a monthly AI budget that comes with Pro. Local and custom models are not supported yet, according to its docs.

In the other app the AI is the author. Every answer worth keeping becomes one of your AI notes, with a title, a one-line summary, an expanded body, and the original question and reply underneath, which are only ever appended to. The model decides the chapter. You pay your own provider through your own key, any of seven: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, Groq, OpenRouter, a local Ollama or your own OpenAI-compatible server.

So the split is about who designs the structure. Capacities wants you to define types such as Book or Meeting, with their properties, before you write much. Here you define nothing, and the AI notes arrange themselves.

## A Monday with a reading list

You are reading a book on soil biology and asking Claude about it along the way.

In Capacities you create a Book object, add the author as a Person, start a page for chapter three and link concepts as you go. The daily note catches loose thoughts. Related Content later shows where you mentioned mycorrhiza without linking it. It is a pleasant way to build a library, and it takes effort.

In the vault app you ask Claude why mycorrhizal fungi help tomatoes and keep the answer. It becomes an AI note under “2.1 Soil life”, links to the existing note on compost because that file exists, and shows up in the backlinks there. Ask the same thing again in a month, and a duplicate check scores the overlap from 0 to 1 and offers a merge. Your follow-up questions go out with the table of contents and up to five relevant notes inside 24,000 characters.

The chat is where every note starts. The answer on screen is saved, titled and filed.

## Capacities is the better pick when

You enjoy designing a knowledge base. Object types, properties and templates are the whole appeal, and nothing comparable exists in a vault that the model arranges for you.

Also choose it if you need a phone app or Linux today, because the other app has neither yet and is not downloadable at all right now. The free Basic plan is generous: unlimited spaces and objects, sync, offline use, full-text search, saving from WhatsApp, Telegram and email. Pro adds tasks, calendar integration, Readwise and Kindle import, a public API and AI Chat Connectors that let ChatGPT or Claude work with a space over MCP.

If your notes are mostly your own reading, writing and planning rather than AI notes from chats, Capacities fits better.

## The better pick for AI notes from daily chats

- You already ask Claude, GPT or Gemini dozens of questions a week, and the answers are the material. Saving one is a click, not a modelling decision.
- You want to choose the model and pay its maker directly, or keep everything offline with Ollama. Capacities picks from its own list and does not run local models yet.
- You want files, not a cloud database: plain `.md` notes on your disk, no sync, no server holding them.
- Cost matters. One plan at 1 USD a month against 119.88 USD a year for Capacities Pro, although with your own key the model usage is billed on top.
- You hand results to others as a PDF or an editable `.pptx` deck in table-of-contents order.

The [AI notes app](https://sumizai.com/) overview walks through the full flow from chat to filed note.

## Moving notes in either direction

Capacities exports to Markdown on every plan, and the importer here reads `.md`, `.markdown`, `.txt` and `.pdf`, single files or a whole folder, one file at a time. That makes this the easiest migration of the lot. Each exported object becomes an AI note with its text kept as the source; properties and object types turn into plain text in that source, and chapters replace the types.

The way back is a ZIP: `base.md` with the table of contents and all notes in `notes/`, with relative links.

## Capacities and SumizAI: quick answers

### Is the Capacities AI included in the free plan?

No. The pricing page lists no AI features on Basic. Pro and Believer include a light monthly AI budget, and a Plus AI add-on (8.33 USD a month, billed yearly) raises it.

### Can I bring my own API key to either app?

Here you must: the app works only with your own key, or a local model. Capacities' AI documentation describes its own budget and model list and does not mention local models.

### Does the vault app have object types or a graph?

No object types, no properties, no graph. AI notes in a vault have chapters, Markdown links and backlinks.

### Where is my data stored?

Capacities: encrypted servers in EU data centres. Here: your Mac. Questions go from the Mac straight to the provider you chose, after you confirm.

### What if I stop paying?

Your vaults still open and export; only new conversations stop. Capacities keeps the free Basic plan for its own users.
