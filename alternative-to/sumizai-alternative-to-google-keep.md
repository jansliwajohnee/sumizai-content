# SumizAI vs Google Keep: a sticky note or a filed answer

Google Keep is the free note app from Google that sits on most Android phones already: colourful cards, checklists, reminders and voice memos, all synced to your Google account. SumizAI does a different job. It takes the answers you get from an AI model and turns them into AI notes with a title, a summary and a place in a table of contents.

So the useful question is which kind of note you are trying to keep. Below: a table checked on 23 October 2026, one ordinary Thursday with both apps, and the cases where Keep is the right pick.

## The two apps side by side

|  | SumizAI | Google Keep |
|---|---|---|
| Who writes the note | The AI model, from your chat; you edit it afterwards | You: typed, dictated or photographed |
| Price | 7 days free, then 1 USD a month, one plan | Free with a Google account |
| Licence | Proprietary subscription | Proprietary, free |
| Platforms | macOS first, coming to the Mac App Store; Windows, iOS and Android in preparation; nothing to download yet | Android, iPhone and iPad (iOS 18 or later), web at keep.google.com |
| Where notes live | Markdown files on your own disk, indexed by a local database | Your Google account, synced in real time |
| Organisation | A table of contents up to six levels deep (`1.`, `1.1`, `a)`, `a.1)`), chapters chosen by the model | Labels, colours, pins and filters |
| Links between notes | Markdown links written by the model; backlinks under each note | None |
| AI | Built in: seven providers on your own API key | The Gemini app can create notes and lists in Keep |
| Reminders, voice, photos | None | Time and location reminders, voice memos transcribed, text read from images |
| Sharing | None; one person per vault | Real-time collaboration on notes and lists |
| Export | ZIP of Markdown, PDF in two levels, editable PowerPoint with 15 themes | Google data download; copy a note to Google Docs |

## One Thursday, two kinds of note

08:10, at the bus stop. You remember that the balcony tomatoes need stakes and that the shop on the corner sells them. Keep is the right tool: a two-line card, a location reminder for the shop, ten seconds with one thumb. An app for AI notes has nothing to offer at that moment.

21:30, at the desk. You ask Claude why the lower leaves are turning yellow and get four paragraphs about nitrogen, watering and early blight. Pasted into a Keep card, they would sit a month later between a parking spot and a Wi-Fi password, untitled.

With the AI note app, the same answer lands in the vault “Home garden” as a note called something like “Yellow lower leaves on tomatoes”: one-line summary, expanded text, and your question with Claude's reply kept word for word underneath. The model files it under the chapter on plant problems. Asked something close in June? Then a second model scores the overlap from 0 to 1 and the app asks whether to merge the two AI notes or keep both.

A finished note in the “Home garden” vault: title, summary, body, then the original chat.

## Pick Google Keep if this is you

Keep wins more often than a product page likes to admit.

- Your notes are short: shopping lists, door codes, a name you must not forget. Keep opens in a second and costs nothing.
- You need reminders by time or by place. This app has no reminders at all.
- You share a list with a partner and want ticks to show up on both phones at once.
- You capture by voice or camera. Keep transcribes voice memos and pulls text out of photos; the Mac app does neither.
- You live on an Android phone. Keep runs there today, while the Mac app has no mobile version and no build you can install right now.

Three of five? Stay with Keep. A free tool that already does its job does not need replacing.

## Pick SumizAI if your best notes are AI notes

The case for switching is narrow. It fits people who ask Claude, GPT or Gemini real questions every day and keep losing the good answers in a scroll of old chats.

- You want the whole answer kept, with its source. The original question and reply stay under every note and are only ever appended to.
- You want structure without filing by hand. In Keep you assign labels; here the model picks a chapter by its number or asks for a new sub-chapter.
- You want notes that point to each other. Links are ordinary Markdown such as `[Early blight](early-blight.md)`, and the model can only link to notes that exist.
- You hand material to other people: a PDF with summaries only or with full notes, or a `.pptx` deck in table-of-contents order.

Keep stores everything in your Google account. The vault is a set of files on your Mac with no sync, and a follow-up question goes straight to the AI provider you chose, after you approve it, with the five most relevant notes at most (24,000 characters).

Plenty of people arrive here from the other side: they left Keep for Obsidian to get Markdown files and links, then found they never had time to write anything. That gap is the subject of the page on [an Obsidian alternative that writes for you](https://sumizai.com/alternative-to/sumizai-alternative-to-obsidian.html). ## Moving notes from Keep, and back out again

There is no Keep importer. The import panel reads `.md`, `.markdown`, `.txt` and `.pdf`, so the shortest route runs through Google Docs: copy the Keep note to Docs, then in Docs choose File > Print and save it as PDF. Import that file or a whole folder of them. Each one becomes a note with its original text attached as the source, and the table of contents is rebuilt after every file.

For a full backup, use Google's data download for Keep. According to Google it contains note text and list items, attachments such as voice recordings and drawings, colours, pinned and archived state, collaborators and your labels. Not every card deserves to become an AI note anyway; a grocery list gains nothing from a summary.

The other direction is short. A vault exports as a ZIP with `base.md` holding the table of contents and the notes in `notes/`.

## Google Keep or an AI notes app: short answers

### Is Google Keep really free?

Yes. Keep comes with a Google account and the App Store lists it as free. Writing AI notes here costs 1 USD a month after a seven-day trial, and your AI provider bills its model usage separately, on your own key.

### Does Keep have AI?

Through Gemini. Google's Gemini help describes the Gemini app creating notes and lists in Keep and adding items to lists you already have. The AI lives in Gemini; Keep itself writes no summaries, files nothing into chapters and does not look for duplicates.

### Can SumizAI record voice notes the way Keep does?

No. There is no recording and no transcription. You type or paste a question, and the note comes out of the chat.

### Is there a phone version yet?

Not yet. The Mac version comes first, through the Mac App Store; Windows, iOS and Android are in preparation. Keep runs on Android, iPhone, iPad and the web today.

### What happens to my notes if I stop paying?

Every vault still opens and exports; the app only stops starting new conversations.
