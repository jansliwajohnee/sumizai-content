# Simplenote or AI notes: who types the first line?

Simplenote is the free plain-text notebook from Automattic, the company behind WordPress: you type a line on your phone and it appears on your laptop a moment later. SumizAI starts from a different end. You ask a model a question, and the answer you want to keep turns into AI notes with a title, a summary and the original chat attached.

So the two apps barely compete. One is a fast pad for your own words, the other a filing clerk for answers that came from Claude, GPT or a local model. Simplenote facts below come from simplenote.com and its help pages, read on 23 October 2026.

One answer from a chat, kept as a note: title, summary, details, and the question and answer word for word underneath.

## A Saturday with seeds, a phone and a chat window

Say it is early March and you are starting tomatoes on the windowsill. At the garden centre you open Simplenote, type "seed compost, 9 cm pots, labels", tick the lines off as you walk, and the list is on the kitchen laptop by the time you get home. Nothing beats that. Three seconds, no thinking.

Back home you ask Claude how deep to sow, what temperature the seeds want and when to pot them on. The answer is long and good. In Simplenote you would copy it, paste it, trim it and pick tags. With AI notes you just keep it: a note called "Sowing tomatoes indoors" appears in the vault "Home garden", with a two-line summary, the full answer and your question underneath, already filed under the chapter about seedlings. Ask again in April about hardening off, and the app checks whether that answer overlaps the March one before saving.

Same Saturday, two different jobs. The shopping list belongs in Simplenote. The growing advice is where AI notes earn their keep.

## Stay with Simplenote when

- You want something free. Simplenote costs nothing, including sync; the other app costs 1 USD a month plus your model usage.
- Your notes live on a phone. Simplenote runs on iOS and Android today, while the chat-to-notes app has no mobile version yet.
- You use Linux or a browser on a shared computer.
- You write lists, codes, addresses, half sentences: text you type faster than you could ask a model about it.
- You share a shopping list with someone at home. Simplenote's Collaborate panel adds a person by email address; AI notes here are for one person.

One more point in Simplenote's favour: every change is backed up, and most of its apps let you view older versions of a note.

## Where AI notes do better than a plain notepad

The case for switching is narrower and very specific. If a large share of what you learn arrives as answers in an AI chat, typing those answers again is the bottleneck, and a faster editor does not remove it.

When you ask something new inside a vault, the question goes out with the vault's table of contents and the full text of up to five relevant notes, within 24,000 characters, and the screen shows which notes were used. A tag cloud cannot do that. Search works by meaning across titles, summaries and text, so "when do seedlings go outside" finds the hardening-off note even if those words are not in it.

Readers comparing Obsidian alternatives ask the same thing about Markdown. The app keeps every note as a `.md` file, which is why it works as an [Obsidian alternative that writes the notes from your chats](https://sumizai.com/alternative-to/sumizai-alternative-to-obsidian.html) while Obsidian can still open the exported vault. For a tour of the whole [AI notes app](https://sumizai.com/), start at the home page.

## Moving notes from Simplenote, and back

Out of Simplenote: on the desktop app choose File → Export Notes. You get a ZIP with one file per note, each named after the note's first line. The help page warns that the Mac App Store build cannot export, so Mac users need the Electron version from GitHub. On Android the export is a `simplenote.json` file, which the importer here does not read.

Into the vault: unpack the ZIP and pick the folder in the import panel. The importer takes `.md`, `.markdown`, `.txt` and `.pdf`, one file at a time, and each file becomes one of your AI notes, with your original text kept as its source. Short lists come out as short notes, so import the long ones and leave the shopping lists where they are.

Out of the app: a ZIP with `base.md` (the table of contents) and a `notes/` folder. Simplenote has no folder import, so you would paste notes in one by one.

## Simplenote questions people ask before switching

### Is Simplenote still free?

Yes. Its home page says apps, backups, syncing and sharing are all completely free.

### Does Simplenote have AI?

Nothing about AI appears on simplenote.com. You write every note yourself.

### Is this an Obsidian alternative or a Simplenote replacement?

Closer to an Obsidian alternative: Markdown files, a vault, links. It replaces Simplenote only for the long answers you would otherwise paste in by hand.

### Can I use both apps?

Easily. Keep quick lists in Simplenote on your phone and let chat answers become AI notes on the Mac. They do not touch each other.

### Does SumizAI sync like Simplenote?

No. Notes stay on the Mac where the vault was created. One subscription covers all your Macs, but there is no server holding notes.

### Can AI notes from the app be published to a link?

No. Simplenote can publish a note to a simp.ly address; here you export a PDF or a PowerPoint deck instead.
