# SumizAI vs Bear: a beautiful editor or a Mac app that does the writing

Bear is a Markdown notes app for Mac, iPhone and iPad, loved for its clean editor, tags with icons and quick wiki-links, with sync and extra exports in a 2.99 USD Bear Pro subscription.

Both apps are Apple-first and both charge a small monthly fee after a 7-day trial. The difference is who types. In Bear it is always you; here a model does it, turning the chat answers you keep into AI notes with a title, a summary and the full exchange as the source. Bear's prices and formats were read on bear.app on 23 October 2026.

Dark mode on the Mac: the table of contents of the demo vault “Home garden”.

## Bear and SumizAI: the facts in one table

|  | SumizAI | Bear |
|---|---|---|
| Who types | A model from your chat; you edit | You |
| Subscription | 1 USD a month after 7 free days, one plan | Bear Pro 2.99 USD a month or 29.99 USD a year, after a 7-day trial |
| Free use | Read and export notes after cancelling | Local notes without sync, document scanning, 3 themes |
| Devices | macOS first, via the Mac App Store soon; Windows, iOS and Android planned | Mac, iPhone, iPad; Bear Web in a browser |
| Storage | Plain `.md` files in a folder, with a local index | A SQLite database inside the app's container |
| Sync | None | iCloud (CloudKit) on Pro, Advanced Data Protection supported |
| Organising | Numbered chapters chosen by the model | Tags with over 250 icons, pinned tags |
| Links | Markdown links to existing files, backlinks under the note | Wiki-links `[[…]]`, also to headings, with aliases |
| AI | Built in; seven providers on your own API key | Not part of the feature list |
| Export | ZIP of `.md`, PDF in two levels, `.pptx` with 15 themes | Free: `.md`, `.txt`, `.rtf`, TextBundle; Pro adds PDF, HTML, DOCX, JPG, ePub |

## Why someone with Bear would add an app for AI notes

Bear is a superb place to write. It does not help you keep what an AI told you, and that is the gap this app fills. Open a vault, ask a question, and the reply that matters lands in the right chapter without you choosing a tag or a title.

Two details make it more than a clipboard. The model links the new note only to files that already exist, using a map of titles it receives, so you never get a link to nowhere. And before saving it checks for near-duplicates, scored 0 to 1 by a second model, which keeps a vault from filling with five versions of one answer.

Later, ask something new: the model gets the table of contents and at most five relevant notes, within 24,000 characters, and the app tells you which ones it read. Your own [AI notes](https://sumizai.com/) become the context for the next answer.

The money side is plain. 1 USD a month, plus whatever Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Groq or OpenRouter charge on your key, or nothing extra with a local Ollama.

## Why Bear stays the better tool for many people

Be honest about what you do all day. If it is writing (journals, drafts, recipes, a novel), Bear wins without a contest.

- Your notes follow you to iPhone and iPad today, synced through iCloud.
- You want to write in a distraction-free editor and format with a tap.
- You scan paper documents and want OCR search inside PDFs and images.
- You lock private notes with encryption.
- You link notes by hand with `[[` and jump straight to a heading.
- You export to DOCX or ePub for an editor or a reader.

This app has none of that: no phone version yet, no encryption of single notes, no rich editor to speak of. It writes, files and exports. That is all.

## One Saturday in the garden

Picture a gardener planning the season. In Bear he types a note called *Tomatoes 2026*, adds a photo of the seed packets, tags it `#garden/tomatoes` and writes a checklist. It reads well. It took twenty minutes.

In the vault “Home garden” he asks Claude when to sow tomatoes indoors for a May planting. Thirty seconds later there is one of his AI notes, titled *Sowing tomatoes indoors*, filed under *2. Vegetables* → *2.1 Tomatoes*, with a summary line, the expanded answer and his question kept below it. When he asks about hardening off seedlings a week later, the app lists the sowing note among the five it sent along with the question.

Bear holds what he wrote. The vault of AI notes holds what he learned by asking. Both have a place on the same Mac.

## Bear to vault, vault to Bear

**From Bear.** On the Mac, select notes (or press ⌘A in the Notes section) and use File → Export notes… (⇧⌘S). Pick Markdown, which works on the free plan. Then open the import panel here and choose the folder. Files go through one by one; each becomes an AI note with Bear's text attached as its source. Wiki-links and tags stay as plain text in that source.

**To Bear.** Export the vault as a ZIP and unpack it. In Bear choose File → Import From → Markdown Folder: it imports every `.md` file in the folder and its subfolders, so `base.md` and `notes/` come in together.

If Bear is not your only editor, the same ZIP opens in Obsidian as a vault, links working. That is why we describe [this Obsidian alternative](https://sumizai.com/alternative-to/sumizai-alternative-to-obsidian.html) as a writer of files, not a place you get locked into.

## Questions from Bear users about AI notes

### Is SumizAI available on iPhone like Bear?

Not yet. The Mac version comes first; iOS, Android and Windows are in preparation.

### Do the AI notes sync through iCloud?

No. There is no sync; a vault lives on the Mac where you created it. One subscription covers all your Macs.

### Does it use Bear-style wiki-links?

No. Links are ordinary Markdown, like `[Sowing tomatoes indoors](sowing-tomatoes-indoors.md)`.

### Which is cheaper?

On the subscription alone, this one: 1 USD a month against 2.99 USD for Bear Pro. Add your AI provider's bill, which depends on the model and how much you ask.

### Are both of them Obsidian alternatives?

Both keep Markdown you can take elsewhere. Bear is an Obsidian alternative for writers; this one fits people whose notes start in AI chats.
