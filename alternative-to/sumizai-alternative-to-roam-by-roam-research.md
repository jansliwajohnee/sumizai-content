# SumizAI vs Roam Research: a graph you weave or a shelf that fills itself

Roam Research calls itself a tool for networked thought, as easy as a document and as powerful as a graph database, and it is built for researchers and writers who connect ideas block by block for years.

My comparison puts it next to a Mac app that does the writing: you hold a conversation with an AI model, and the answers you keep become AI notes, each with a title, a summary and the original question and reply stored beneath. Roam's prices and platforms come from roamresearch.com, read on 23 October 2026.

A list, not a graph: each note shows its title and one sentence of summary.

## Roam Research and the AI notes app in ten rows

|  | SumizAI | Roam Research |

| Cost | 1 USD a month after a 7-day trial; AI usage paid to the provider | Pro 15 USD a month or 165 USD a year; Believer 500 USD for 5 years |

## Roam Research wins when the thinking is yours

Roam was made for one kind of work, and for that work this app is the wrong tool.

If you are writing a book or a thesis and the value lies in the links you make yourself (this quote supports that claim, this idea contradicts last month's), Roam's graph is the product. A machine that files answers into chapters would flatten exactly the part you care about.

- You collaborate: Pro includes unlimited collaborators and public graphs.
- You build on Roam with its API and apps.
- You work on Linux, Windows or an Android phone right now.
- You prefer a 31-day trial and a one-off 500 USD Believer plan to a monthly bill.

None of that exists here. No graph view, no block references, no shared graphs.

## Tuesday in the library, Tuesday in a chat

Imagine a historian spending a Tuesday on how the printing press spread through Europe. In Roam she writes bullets as she reads, links each city to its own page, and quotes a block about Venice into her chapter draft. Two hours of reading become forty linked blocks, every one typed by her.

Same Tuesday, different habit: she asks Gemini why Venice had so many presses by 1500. The reply is saved as one of her AI notes under *4. Trade* → *4.2 Printing*, titled and summarised, with the full question and answer kept word for word below. The model adds a Markdown link to the note on paper mills, which already exists, and the paper note lists the new one among its backlinks.

Roam gives her a web of her own thoughts. The vault of AI notes gives her a sorted shelf of what she asked and was told.

## A vault of AI notes wins when the answers come from AI

Picture the opposite reader: you ask GPT, Claude or Gemini a dozen questions a day and keep none of the answers. For you, an [AI notes app](https://sumizai.com/) fixes the real problem; a better outliner does not.

The price gap is real too: 1 USD a month here against 15 USD for Roam Pro. You pay the model provider separately, on your own key with Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, Groq or OpenRouter, or nothing at all with a local Ollama.

And the app remembers for you. Before a new note is saved, full-text and trigram search find look-alikes and a second model rates them from 0 to 1, so the same question asked twice ends up as one note with an appended answer. Later questions go out with the table of contents and at most five fitting AI notes, never more than 24,000 characters, and you see which ones were used.

## From a Roam graph to a vault, and the other way

**Out of Roam.** Open the ••• menu, choose Export All and save the archive. The importer here reads `.md`, `.markdown`, `.txt` and `.pdf`, not JSON, so the graph needs a stop in between. Obsidian's free Roam importer turns that JSON export into a folder of Markdown files; point the import panel at the folder and each file becomes one of your AI notes, with its original text attached as the source.

That detour is also a fair test. If you end up liking the Markdown copy in Obsidian, read how [SumizAI works as an Obsidian alternative](https://sumizai.com/alternative-to/sumizai-alternative-to-obsidian.html) and alongside it.

**Into Roam.** A vault downloads as a ZIP of plain Markdown with `base.md` on top. I could not check Roam's import options without an account, so try a few files before a whole vault.

## Roam users ask about AI notes and Obsidian alternatives

### Does this app have block references like Roam?

No. The smallest unit is the whole note, and links between notes are ordinary Markdown links.

### Is there a graph view of my AI notes?

No. You browse a numbered table of contents and the backlinks listed under each note.

### Can I use it with my team, as with Roam's collaborators?

No. A vault belongs to one person, with no sync and no server copy of your notes.

### Why is it so much cheaper than Roam?

Because you bring your own model. The 1 USD covers the app; the model bill goes to your provider, and the app adds nothing on top.

### Should a Roam user look at Obsidian alternatives at all?

Only if your notes start in AI chats and you are tired of copying them. For linked thinking you type yourself, Roam is the better home.
