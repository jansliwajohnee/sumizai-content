# SumizAI vs DEVONthink: one keeps your documents, the other keeps your answers

DEVONthink is the heavyweight document manager for the Mac: PDFs, emails, web pages, scans and notes in local databases, with search, smart rules, scripting and, in the Pro edition, generative AI. SumizAI deals with one kind of material only, the answers you get from AI models, and turns them into AI notes with their source attached.

Both run on a Mac and both keep data on your own disk, which makes this comparison less about privacy and more about what you collect. DEVONthink facts were read on devontechnologies.com on 23 October 2026.

A finished note, “Sowing tomatoes indoors”: title, summary, expanded text, and the original question and answer below.

## A research afternoon, done twice

Say you are writing a report on heat pumps. You have 40 PDFs from manufacturers, a few scanned brochures, a dozen saved web pages and a long email thread with an installer. DEVONthink swallows all of it. Pro recognises the text in the scans, the AI suggests a group for each file, and a smart rule tags anything mentioning “COP”. Two hours later the pile is a searchable archive.

Then you start asking questions. Why does efficiency drop below minus ten degrees? What does an air-to-water unit need that a split system does not? Each answer from Claude is worth keeping, and none of them is a document.

That gap is the whole reason this comparison exists. In the AI notes app every useful answer becomes a note in a chapter such as `2. Efficiency`, with a one-line summary and the exact question and reply underneath. You can also feed it the same PDFs: the importer turns each one into one of your AI notes and keeps the original text as its source.

## Where DEVONthink is the stronger tool

For anything that arrives as a file, DEVONthink is far ahead, and it has had years to get there.

- Every format you deal with: texts, PDFs, websites, images, emails, spreadsheets, notes in Markdown, plain text or rich text.
- Scanner integration and OCR (Pro and Server), so paper becomes searchable.
- Automation with smart groups, smart rules, AppleScript and JavaScript for Automation.
- Sync between Macs over the local network, a file server, iCloud, Dropbox or WebDAV, optionally encrypted with AES-256.
- iPhone and iPad through DEVONthink To Go, included with Pro.
- Revision-proof databases for records you are required to keep unchanged.

It also lets you pick your AI model, online or local, and connects databases to apps such as Claude through MCP. If you live in documents, buy DEVONthink and stop reading here.

## What AI notes add to a document archive

DEVONthink's assistant summarises a document you already have. The note app starts one step earlier, at the conversation, so the thing you keep is the answer itself.

Each new question goes out with the vault's table of contents and the full text of up to five of the most relevant AI notes, never more than 24,000 characters, and the screen shows which ones were used. Before a note is saved, a second model rates it against similar ones from 0 to 1; above the vault's threshold you decide whether to merge, and merging only ever appends. Links between notes are plain Markdown, pointing only at files that exist, with backlinks under each note.

The money side is very different. A DEVONthink Pro licence costs 199 USD up front. This [AI notes app](https://sumizai.com/) costs 1 USD a month plus what Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Groq or OpenRouter charge you directly.

And for readers who arrived from the Obsidian side: SumizAI is also an [Obsidian alternative that writes AI notes from your chats](https://sumizai.com/alternative-to/sumizai-alternative-to-obsidian.html). Its exported folder opens in Obsidian unchanged, which also makes it easy to bring into DEVONthink. Of the Obsidian alternatives on this site, it is the one built around a chat window.

## Using both on one Mac

These two fit together better than most pairs. Keep the archive in DEVONthink and the AI notes in the vault.

To send material from DEVONthink, export documents in their original format: Markdown and text notes stay `.md` or `.txt`, and PDFs stay PDFs. The importer takes `.md`, `.markdown`, `.txt` and `.pdf`, single files or a whole folder, processes them one by one and rebuilds the table of contents after each. RTF is not accepted, so convert it to plain text first.

In the other direction, export the vault as a ZIP. Inside you get `base.md` with the table of contents and a `notes/` folder; unpack it and add the folder to a DEVONthink database, or index it in place, since DEVONthink can reference external files without moving them.

## Questions about DEVONthink and AI notes

### Does DEVONthink Standard include AI?

No. The editions table lists generative AI integration, OCR and DEVONthink To Go for Pro and Server only.

### Is DEVONthink a subscription?

No. You buy a licence once; it includes a year of updates, which you can extend, and the app stays fully usable when updates end.

### Which one should hold my PDFs?

DEVONthink. Importing PDFs into the note app works, but each file becomes one of your AI notes, not an archived document.

### Can I use my own model in both?

Yes. DEVONthink lets you choose an online or local model; the note app supports seven providers on your key, local Ollama among them.

### Do I need an account?

For SumizAI, yes, an e-mail account; one subscription covers all your Macs.
