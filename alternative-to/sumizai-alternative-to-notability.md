# SumizAI vs Notability: the pen and the microphone, or the chat window

Notability, made by Ginger Labs, is where students and note-takers write with a pencil on an iPad, mark up imported documents and record audio next to the page. SumizAI has none of that. It starts from a conversation with an AI model and keeps the answers you choose as AI notes: a title, a summary, the expanded text and the original exchange underneath.

So the overlap is smaller than the category label suggests. I checked Notability’s plans, platforms and export formats on its own site and help centre on 23 October 2026; everything below about it comes from there.

The starting point here is a chat, not a blank page or a recording.

## A study day that needs both tools

Picture a biology student. At 9:00 she opens Notability, imports the lecturer’s slides, writes over them with an Apple Pencil and lets the app record the 90-minute lecture. Later she taps a word in her handwriting and hears what was said at that moment. No app on this page beats that.

At 20:00 she is stuck on the difference between mitosis and meiosis and asks Claude. Three follow-up questions later the explanation finally clicks. In Notability that answer would have to be pasted in by hand, and it rarely is. In an AI notes app the explanation she marks becomes a note in the vault “Biology”, filed under chapter `3.2` (cell division), with her questions kept word for word as the source.

Next week, before the exam, she asks a new question in the same vault. The model receives the table of contents and the five most relevant notes, within 24,000 characters, and answers with her own earlier material in view.

Different hours of the day. Different tools.

## Pick Notability when the input is ink or sound

This is the larger group of people, and Notability is plainly the better tool for them:

- you write by hand on an iPad and want that writing searchable;
- you annotate PDFs: slides, papers, planners, contracts;
- you record lectures or meetings and want the transcript tied to your notes;
- you need the notes on an iPhone, an Android tablet or in a browser today;
- you want flashcards and quizzes generated from class material.

None of that exists in this AI notes app. There is no recorder, no pen layer and no mobile version yet.

## Pick AI notes when the input is a conversation with a model

The opposite case. You learn by asking Claude, GPT or Gemini, and the best explanations of the week are scattered across old chats. Here every kept answer gets a title and a summary, lands in a chapter, and is checked against what you already have: full-text and trigram search find candidates, a second model scores the overlap from 0 to 1, and above the vault’s threshold you decide whether to merge or keep both. A merge only appends.

Links come from the model too, as ordinary Markdown links to files that already exist, with backlinks listed under each note. Your AI notes stay readable without the app: they are `.md` files, not a proprietary format.

One more difference that matters for students on a budget: the subscription is 1 USD a month, and you pay your AI provider directly for the tokens you use.

People who already keep notes in Markdown sometimes come here looking for an Obsidian alternative with AI built in; the exported vault opens in Obsidian unchanged. The full argument is on the [Obsidian alternative](https://sumizai.com/alternative-to/sumizai-alternative-to-obsidian.html) page.

## Getting material from one app into the other

**Notability → Mac app.** Export a note as PDF (or set PDF as the auto-backup format). The importer accepts `.pdf` along with `.md`, `.markdown` and `.txt`, so a typed note or an exported transcript comes in as one of your AI notes, with the original text kept as its source. RTF is not on the import list. Pages that are pure ink give the importer no typed text to read, so try one handwritten note before a whole semester.

**Mac app → Notability.** Export the vault of AI notes, or one chapter of it, to PDF: either summaries only or the full notes. Import that PDF into Notability and write on it with the pencil. For revision that pairing works well: the model drafts the study sheet, you annotate it.

## Short answers about Notability and SumizAI

### Can SumizAI transcribe my lectures?

No. It has no audio recording and no transcription. Notability does both, with live transcription on its Pro plan.

### Does the Mac app read handwriting?

No. Its AI notes are built from text: chat answers and imported `.md`, `.markdown`, `.txt` or `.pdf` files.

### Is there an iPad version?

Not yet. The first release is for macOS through the Mac App Store; iOS is in preparation. If the iPad is your main device, Notability fits better today.

### Whose AI is used for the notes?

Yours. You add your own API key for one of the seven providers, requests go straight from your Mac to that provider after you agree, and nothing is added to the provider’s price. Notability’s AI features come with its plans instead.

### Can I use both?

Yes, and for students that is the natural split: Notability in the lecture hall, AI notes from chats in the evening, PDF export as the bridge.
