## How the two tools think about notes

Obsidian assumes the thinking happens while you write. You open an empty page, type, link it to other pages, and over months a web of notes grows. The tool stays out of your way, which is exactly why people love it.

This Obsidian alternative assumes the thinking already happened somewhere else: in a conversation with Claude or GPT. The good answer is there, on screen. Copying it into a notes app works for about three days and then the habit dies. So the app does the copying, and more: it writes a title and a one-line summary, keeps the full question and answer underneath as the source, and files the finished AI note into a chapter.

That is the real difference behind every row of the table. An Obsidian alternative that also wants you to write everything yourself would just be another editor; this one changes who holds the pen.

## A week with each tool

Picture an ordinary Tuesday, once with Obsidian and once with an Obsidian alternative that writes for you. Claude has just explained how Postgres picks an index. In Obsidian you open the vault, create a note, paste the useful part, give it a title, choose a folder and add two links. Ten minutes, if you do it at all.

In SumizAI you ask the same question inside a vault called “Work”. The answer streams in. A few seconds later one of your AI notes appears with a title, a one-line summary, the expanded answer and your original question underneath. It is already in the chapter about databases, next to the AI notes you made in June, and if the two overlap, the app asks whether to merge them.

By Friday the difference is plain. The Obsidian vault holds the notes you had the energy to write. The other one holds every answer you chose to keep, as AI notes you can search, read and export.

Neither result is wrong. They suit different people, and five working days is the honest test for any Obsidian alternative.

## When Obsidian is the better choice

Stay with Obsidian if any of these sound like you:

- You enjoy writing notes, and the writing is how you think. An Obsidian alternative that writes for you would take away the part you value.
- You rely on plugins: Dataview, Templater, Kanban, spaced repetition. This one has no plugin system at all.
- You use the graph view or Canvas to see your ideas. The app shows chapters and links, not a map.
- You need Linux, or your notes on a phone today. The Mac version comes first; the other platforms are still in preparation.
- You want sync with version history across devices. Obsidian Sync does that; this alternative to Obsidian does not sync.
- You want to pay nothing. Obsidian is free; the subscription here costs 1 USD a month plus your AI usage.

None of these is a small point. For a large share of Obsidian users the honest answer is: keep it.

## Using both: SumizAI writes, Obsidian reads

You don't have to choose. This Obsidian alternative keeps every note as a plain Markdown file, so a vault can live in both worlds. Download it as a ZIP, unpack it anywhere, and in Obsidian choose “Open folder as vault”. The links between notes are relative Markdown links, so they work without the app.

A practical split: let SumizAI collect and file what comes out of AI chats, then browse, annotate and connect those AI notes in Obsidian with your own plugins. Re-export when the vault has grown. Treat the Obsidian copy as a reading copy, because edits made there do not flow back.

The same notes are plain .md files, so Obsidian reads them as they are.

## Moving notes between them

From SumizAI to Obsidian

: ZIP download, unpack, open as vault. Nothing to convert. Links written by the app are ordinary Markdown links like

[Soil mix](soil-mix.md)

, not

[[wiki-links]]

; Obsidian handles both.

From Obsidian to SumizAI

: open the import panel and pick files or the whole vault folder. The importer accepts

.md

,

.markdown

,

.txt

and

.pdf

and processes them one file at a time, rebuilding the table of contents after each note. Every file becomes one of your AI notes, with its original text attached as the source. Expect a rewrite rather than a mirror: your folder structure is replaced by chapters, and Obsidian-only syntax such as Dataview blocks stays as plain text inside the source.

A vault of a few hundred files takes a while, and every file costs a model call on your own key. Start with one folder and see whether you like the result before importing everything.

## Questions about switching to an Obsidian alternative

### Is SumizAI a free Obsidian alternative?

No. It is free for seven days and then costs 1 USD a month, plus whatever your AI provider charges for the model. Obsidian itself is free to use, so if price is the only reason you are looking at an Obsidian alternative, Obsidian already wins.

### Is there an Obsidian alternative with AI built in?

SumizAI is one. The AI is the core of the app rather than an add-on: seven providers, your own key, and the model writes, files and links the notes. In Obsidian you get AI through community plugins that you choose and configure yourself.

### Can I open my SumizAI vault in Obsidian?

Yes. Download the vault as a ZIP, unpack it and open the folder as a vault in Obsidian. You get `base.md` with the table of contents and every note in `notes/`, with working relative links.

### Can SumizAI import my Obsidian vault?

It can import the files: pick single notes or the whole folder, and the importer processes `.md`, `.markdown`, `.txt` and `.pdf` one at a time. Each file becomes one of your AI notes, with the original text kept as its source. It is a rewrite into the app's format, not a byte-for-byte copy of your vault.

### Does SumizAI sync between devices like Obsidian Sync?

No. There is no sync and no server holding your notes. One subscription works on all your Macs, but each vault lives on the disk where you created it.

### Which Obsidian alternative should a developer pick?

If you ask Claude or GPT about code all day and lose the good answers in old chats, this Obsidian alternative fits. If you want a hackable editor with templates, Dataview queries and a plugin for everything, that is Obsidian, and no Obsidian alternative matches its plugin ecosystem.
